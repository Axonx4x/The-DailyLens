Students Develop Low-Cost Solar Irrigation for Rural Communities

A team of Malawian engineering students from MUBAS has created an affordable solar-powered irrigation device to help smallholder farmers in Dowa and Mchinji cultivate crops during the dry season.

By Charity Ndhlovu, Lead Investigative Journalist • Apr 21, 2025 • Lilongwe Bureau • 5 min read

In many rural areas across Central Malawi, unpredictable rainfall patterns have historically left smallholder farming families vulnerable during the dry winter cropping months. Now, a group of final-year engineering students from the Malawi University of Business and Applied Sciences (MUBAS) in Blantyre has engineered a homegrown solution: a portable, solar-powered drip-irrigation pump assembled largely from locally sourced and recycled materials.

The students say the project emerged during a community field visit to farming cooperatives in Dowa and Mchinji. “We saw farmers spending significant portions of their modest income on expensive diesel fuel just to pump water from boreholes and riverbeds,” explains lead student innovator Chisomo Banda. “We asked ourselves: why can’t we harness Malawi’s abundant sunshine to power zero-emission, low-cost irrigation?”

Building a Resilient Solution for Malawian Soils

Unlike imported solar kits that carry prohibitive purchase tags and require specialized foreign replacement components, the team designed their prototype around standardized solar cells and local PVC fittings readily stocked in district trading centres. The unit generates sufficient electrical wattage to lift water from depths of up to 25 metres, feeding gravity-assisted drip lines that nourish up to two acres of high-value horticulture crops like tomatoes, onions, and cabbages.

“Our goal was to ensure that a farmer in Dowa doesn’t need to wait weeks for an imported spare part. Innovation in Malawi must be repairable by the people who rely on it daily.” — Chisomo Banda, Student Project Lead (MUBAS)

Working alongside agricultural extension officers from the Ministry of Agriculture, the university innovators conducted extensive field testing across three agricultural districts. Local village youth were trained in routine electrical maintenance and solar panel cleaning, establishing immediate local ownership of the equipment.

Field Testing Benchmarks Across Malawian Pilot Communities

Over a four-month rigorous pilot deployment, the engineering team recorded irrigation throughput, battery lifecycle metrics, and fuel savings across three agricultural test sites. The performance findings are summarized in the table below:

Table 1.1: MUBAS Solar Irrigation Prototype Field Trial Results (Central Malawi)
Pilot District Farming Households Daily Output (Litres) Peak Solar Wattage Monthly Diesel Savings
Dowa Community Scheme 190 3,450 L 350 W MK 145,000 / farm
Mchinji Rural Center 145 2,800 L 320 W MK 120,000 / farm
Salima Lakeside Cooperative 120 2,200 L 300 W MK 95,000 / farm

Scaling Nationwide and Open-Source Schematics

Following the success of the pilot scheme, the National Commission for Science and Technology (NCST) in Lilongwe has awarded the student team seed capital to manufacture 50 additional demonstration units for agricultural technical colleges across Malawi. Furthermore, the students are publishing the blueprints freely under an open hardware license so rural vocational training institutes from Karonga to Nsanje can train a new generation of solar agro-technicians.

For Malawian smallholders confronting climate change, this youth-driven breakthrough offers a tangible glimpse of an equitable, resilient agricultural future powered by homegrown innovation.
