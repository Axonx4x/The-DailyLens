National Youth Civic Forum: Malawian Student Delegates Debate Development Policy in Lilongwe

Over 200 elected student delegates from all 28 districts in Malawi assembled near Capital Hill to propose legislative reforms on tertiary education funding, youth employment quotas, and climate adaptation financing.

By David Banda, Political Correspondent • Apr 22, 2025 • Lilongwe Bureau • 5 min read

In an unprecedented gathering of student leadership, the National Youth Civic Forum opened its plenary sessions on Tuesday morning in Lilongwe, bringing together elected university councilors, secondary school prefects, and rural youth cooperative leaders from Chitipa to Nsanje.

Presided over by non-partisan civic moderators, the summit provides young Malawians with a direct formal platform to scrutinize national budget allocations, review civil service hiring quotas, and advocate for sustainable public education subsidies.

Focus on Tertiary Loan Schemes and Inclusivity

The primary motion debated on the floor centered on restructuring the Higher Education Students’ Loans and Grants Board (HESLGB). Delegates presented a comprehensive petition urging the government to expand loan coverage to vocational technical trainees and eliminate bureaucratic application processing delays.

“Youth are not merely the future of Malawi—we are its present driving force. More than 60 percent of our population is under thirty years old; our national legislation must reflect our lived economic realities.” — Chimwemwe Mhango, Youth Forum Lead Delegate (Mzimba District)

A delegation of Members of Parliament from the Parliamentary Committee on Education attended the afternoon session, pledging to introduce three youth-drafted amendments to the Education Act during the forthcoming sitting of the National Assembly.
