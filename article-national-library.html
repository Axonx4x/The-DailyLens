National Library Service Expands Free Digital Archiving in Zomba

The National Library Service of Malawi has unveiled fifty public high-speed digital research terminals and digitized historical manuscripts in Zomba, democratizing access to academic journals.

By Charity Ndhlovu, Lead Investigative Journalist • Apr 20, 2025 • Zomba Bureau • 4 min read

In the historic university town of Zomba, the National Library Service (NLS) of Malawi has completed an extensive modernization initiative designed to provide students, independent scholars, and citizens with free, unrestricted access to global research repositories.

The upgraded branch now features high-speed optic fibre connectivity, fifty public computer workstations, and a high-resolution scanning repository dedicated to preserving fragile Malawian newspapers, colonial-era gazettes, and indigenous oral history recordings.

Safeguarding Cultural Memory and Scientific Papers

Before this upgrade, university students at the nearby University of Malawi (Chancellor College) frequently struggled to access paywalled international engineering and medical research papers. Through consortium subscriptions negotiated by the Ministry of Education, library cardholders can now download peer-reviewed papers at no charge.

“Libraries are the ultimate equalizers in a democratic society. No student in Zomba should be held back from academic excellence simply because they cannot afford an individual journal fee.” — Dr. Joyce Matenje, Chief National Librarian

The digital repository has recorded over 12,000 document downloads in its first month of operation, serving researchers across all three regions of the country.
