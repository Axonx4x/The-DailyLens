MUBAS Innovation Hub Opens Advanced Renewable Energy Laboratory

The state-of-the-art facility in Blantyre will empower Malawian engineering students and researchers to design, test, and manufacture localized solar, micro-hydro, and biomass clean-energy systems.

By Charity Ndhlovu, Lead Investigative Journalist • Apr 22, 2025 • Blantyre Bureau • 4 min read

In a major milestone for scientific development in Malawi, the Malawi University of Business and Applied Sciences (MUBAS) in Blantyre officially inaugurated its Advanced Renewable Energy and Micro-Grid Laboratory on Tuesday morning. The cutting-edge research facility aims to accelerate industrial prototyping of clean power systems tailored to rural Malawian terrain.

Equipped with precision solar cell testing chambers, power-line harmonics analyzers, and high-capacity battery testing benches, the centre bridges the gap between theoretical classroom learning and field deployment across Malawian off-grid communities.

Tackling National Energy Challenges with Homegrown Talent

Malawi currently faces an acute national electrification deficit, with only approximately 15 percent of the national population connected to the centralized grid. In rural areas, this figure falls below 5 percent. The MUBAS laboratory was specifically designed to foster decentralized micro-utilities that harness river run-off, photovoltaic panels, and agricultural biomass.

“We cannot wait for solutions to be imported from overseas when our own engineering graduates possess the technical intellect to design durable, cost-effective power solutions right here in Blantyre.” — Associate Professor Tamara Gondwe, Dean of Engineering at MUBAS

The hub has already enrolled 45 undergraduate and postgraduate researchers who are developing off-grid energy storage units using recycled vehicle lithium cells and lead-acid batteries, drastically cutting fabrication overheads.

Laboratory Specifications & Research Capacities

The facility was constructed through a partnership between the Ministry of Energy, the National Commission for Science and Technology (NCST), and international clean-technology endowments. The breakdown of core laboratory sections is detailed below:

Table 2.1: Key Testing Facilities at MUBAS Renewable Energy Lab (Blantyre)
Research Section Equipment Focus Testing Capacity Intended Community Beneficiaries
Solar PV Diagnostics Solar Simulators & IV Curve Tracers Panels up to 600W rating Rural clinics, school lighting, household kits
Micro-Hydro Simulation Variable-flow water flume testing tank Turbines from 2kW to 50kW Tea estates and mountain river villages in Thyolo & Mulanje
Agri-Biomass Gasification Combustion calorimetry & gas filters 100 kg / day crop waste Tobacco and maize processing cooperatives in Kasungu

Incubating Student Startups and Commercialization

Beyond academic research, the Innovation Hub features an enterprise incubator. Three student-led ventures have already qualified for commercialization grants to fabricate smart prepaid solar meters that accept local mobile money transfers via Airtel Money and Mpamba.

As the country pursues its Malawi 2063 vision of becoming an inclusive, self-reliant industrialized nation, facilities like the MUBAS laboratory prove that youth-led engineering is vital to achieving widespread energy sovereignty.
