Lilongwe Entrepreneurs Turn Digital Skills Into Agri-Tech Ventures

Young Malawian tech founders in Lilongwe are developing mobile inventory and trade matching platforms that link smallholder soya and legume growers directly with industrial food processors.

By Charity Ndhlovu, Lead Investigative Journalist • Apr 20, 2025 • Lilongwe Bureau • 5 min read

In the vibrant commercial heart of Lilongwe, a new generation of university computer science and agribusiness graduates is bypassing conventional corporate job tracks to build homegrown digital enterprises. Their focus: solving one of Malawi’s most enduring economic riddles—the fractured market linkage between rural smallholder harvest yields and urban commodity buyers.

Historically, smallholder farmers across Kasungu, Mchinji, and Dedza have been forced to sell high-value harvests like soya, groundnuts, and kidney beans to roving middlemen at steep discounts. Now, platforms developed by local software teams are flipping the power dynamic through USSD mobile marketplaces and SMS auction boards that function on basic feature phones.

Eliminating Middlemen Through Transparent Mobile Pricing

Founded by 25-year-old developer Kondwani Mwale and agronomist Tadala Chirwa, the “Chirambo Agro-Connect” platform aggregates harvest forecasts from over 3,200 smallholders. When a commercial flour mill or edible oil factory in Blantyre requires 50 metric tonnes of non-GMO soya, the platform dispatches SMS purchase orders directly to registered farming clubs.

“Transparency is our most valuable asset. When a farmer knows the verified wholesale price at the Kanengo Industrial Area in Lilongwe, they cannot be shortchanged at the farm gate.” — Kondwani Mwale, Co-founder of Chirambo Agro-Connect

Payment settlements are executed instantly through mobile money services, ensuring farmers receive immediate cash into their Airtel Money or Mpamba wallets without travelling long distances to commercial banks.

A recent field review conducted by The DailyLens examined transaction records across four agricultural cooperatives in Central Malawi. The findings illustrate a decisive rise in net household revenues:

Attracting Venture Capital and Regional Expansion

The success of Lilongwe’s tech ecosystem has captured regional attention. Early-stage venture capital funds operating across Southern Africa are providing seed capital to help local developers add automated truck dispatching and humidity monitoring sensors inside cooperative storage warehouses.

As these digital ventures flourish, they showcase how Malawian youth are using practical software engineering to drive genuine rural prosperity.
