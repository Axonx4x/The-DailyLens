Digital Literacy Drive Empowers Learners Across Kasungu & Dedza

Solar-powered mobile computer labs and weekend coding workshops bring digital learning and web skills to secondary school learners in rural Central Malawi.

By Verena Likupe, Senior Education & Tech Reporter • Oct 22, 2026 • Lilongwe Bureau • Verified Journalism
4 min read
Text Size:

For decades, students in rural Community Day Secondary Schools (CDSS) across Malawi sat for national MSCE examinations having studied computer science concepts strictly through textbook diagrams without touching a physical keyboard. That digital divide is rapidly closing in Kasungu and Dedza, thanks to a pioneering solar-powered digital education rollout spearheaded by university student volunteers and local headteachers.

Using refurbished low-power Raspberry Pi microcomputers paired with compact monitors and rooftop photovoltaic solar panels, fifteen secondary schools without grid electricity now operate fully functioning thirty-seat ICT computer classrooms.

Offline Encyclopedias and Practical Programming

Recognizing that costly mobile cellular data limits internet browsing in remote areas, the program utilizes offline educational servers loaded with the Khan Academy, Wikipedia in Chichewa and English, digital chemistry labs, and Python coding tutorials.

“The moment our students saw code they had written produce a calculation on screen, their entire world expanded. They realized technology is not magic from overseas—it is a skill they can command.” — Mercy Tembo, Headteacher at Kasungu North CDSS

Female student participation has surged, with girls outnumbering boys in weekend web-development circles in Dedza.

School Enrollment and Exam Improvements

The Ministry of Education monitored student performance before and after the lab installations. The data demonstrates remarkable gains:

Table 5.1: Impact of Solar Computer Labs on Science & Maths Pass Rates (Kasungu & Dedza)
Participating Secondary School District Enrolled Students Baseline STEM Pass Rate Current STEM Pass Rate
Chilanga CDSS Kasungu 240 44.5% 78.2%
Mayani Day Secondary Dedza 210 39.0% 74.6%
Santhe Community School Kasungu 190 42.1% 81.0%

Organizers plan to expand the initiative to thirty additional rural community schools across Northern and Southern regions over the next twelve months.
