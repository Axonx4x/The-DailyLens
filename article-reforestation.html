Malawian Youth Lead Reforestation Drive on Mount Mulanje Slopes

University students, forestry scholars, and community volunteers have planted over 50,000 indigenous Mulanje cedar and acacia saplings to safeguard critical water catchment basins in Southern Malawi.

By Charity Ndhlovu, Lead Investigative Journalist • Apr 18, 2025 • Mulanje Bureau • 5 min read

Rising dramatically above the emerald tea plains of Southern Malawi, the granite massifs of Mount Mulanje constitute the nation’s most vital ecological biosphere. In recent years, illegal charcoal burning and timber poaching have threatened the endangered Mulanje cedar (Widdringtonia whytei) and caused severe soil erosion during cyclone seasons.

In response, a vibrant grassroots coalition of over 400 youth volunteers from the University of Malawi, MUST, and neighboring village development committees has spent the past three months establishing community tree nurseries across the mountain slopes.

Community-Run Tree Nurseries and Drone Monitoring

Rather than relying solely on sporadic annual tree-planting days, the coalition established nine permanent community nurseries managed by local youth. Innovative student geographers from Lilongwe are using low-cost camera drones to map seedling survival rates and track soil moisture retention along critical stream channels.

“Planting a tree is merely the first ten seconds of conservation. The real work is nurturing that seedling for five years until it stabilizes the mountain soil and restores the freshwater river basin.” — Alinafe Kumwenda, Youth Conservation Team Lead

The initiative is expanding its methodology to the Dzalanyama Forest Reserve bordering Lilongwe, securing drinking water catchments that supply over one million residents in the capital.
